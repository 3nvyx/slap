## 1. FSRS: memory model, formulas, desired retention, versions, and guidance for a fixed exam date

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the version shipped in Anki (26.09.3, fsrs-rs 6.6.2) and in ts-fsrs is still **FSRS-6** (21 parameters, trainable forgetting-curve decay); FSRS-7 (34 parameters, fractional-day intervals) exists in the benchmark repo and is described by its co-developer as "the final version", but I found no evidence it has been merged into Anki or ts-fsrs. FSRS itself has no exam-date mode: the documented exam tools are (a) the desired-retention setting, (b) a maximum-interval cap, (c) the Helper add-on's Advance, and (d) filtered decks; everything more specific ("raise retention to 0.95 two weeks out") is forum advice.

### Cited Findings

**Memory model (DSR)**
- [DOC] Retrievability R = "the probability that the person can successfully recall a particular piece of information at a given moment"; Stability S = "the time, in days, required for R to decrease from 100% to 90%"; Difficulty D = "how difficult it is to increase memory stability after a review". — [ABC of FSRS wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)
- [DOC] FSRS-6 uses 21 parameters; default parameters were derived from "several hundred million reviews from ~10k users"; older versions use only the first review of each day, FSRS-6 uses all reviews. — [ABC of FSRS wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)
- [DOC] The wiki moved in 2026 from `fsrs4anki/wiki` to `awesome-fsrs/wiki`; old URLs show a "page has moved" notice. — [old wiki page](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/wiki/The-Algorithm)

**FSRS-6 formulas (current)** — all from [The Algorithm wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm) unless noted. G = grade (1 Again, 2 Hard, 3 Good, 4 Easy).
- Forgetting curve (trainable decay w20): `R(t,S) = (1 + factor * t/S)^(-w20)`, with `factor = 0.9^(-1/w20) - 1`, so R(S,S) = 90%.
- Same-day (short-term) review: `S' = S * e^(w17*(G-3+w18)) * S^(-w19)`. Stability "grows faster when small and slower when large"; SInc >= 1 should hold for G >= 2.
- FSRS-6 inherits everything else from FSRS-5 / 4.5 / v4:
  - Initial stability: `S0(G) = w[G-1]`.
  - Initial difficulty (FSRS-5+): `D0(G) = w4 - e^(w5*(G-1)) + 1` (w4 = D0(1)).
  - Difficulty update with linear damping (FSRS-5+): `dD = -w6*(G-3)`; `D' = D + dD*(10-D)/9`; mean reversion `D'' = w7*D0(4) + (1-w7)*D'` (target is D0(4); earlier versions used D0(3)).
  - Stability after successful recall: `S'_r = S * ( e^(w8) * (11-D) * S^(-w9) * (e^(w10*(1-R)) - 1) * w15[if G=2] * w16[if G=4] + 1 )`. SInc decreases as D or S increases, increases as R decreases, and is always >= 1 on success.
  - Stability after a lapse: `S'_f = w11 * D^(-w12) * ((S+1)^(w13) - 1) * e^(w14*(1-R))`.
  - Next interval (form given for FSRS-4.5): `I(r,S) = (S/FACTOR) * (r^(1/DECAY) - 1)`; at r = 0.9 the interval equals S.
- [DOC] Post-lapse stability is additionally capped by the pre-lapse stability with a min() term; w20 ranges 0.1–0.8 and is "usually below 0.2"; D is clamped to 1–10; at desired retention 90% the interval equals stability before fuzz; FSRS-6 is "available in Anki since 25.07". — [Expertium, "A technical explanation of FSRS"](https://expertium.github.io/Algorithm.html)
- [DOC] FSRS-6 default parameters: `[0.212, 1.2931, 2.3065, 8.2956, 6.4133, 0.8334, 3.0194, 0.001, 1.8722, 0.1666, 0.796, 1.4835, 0.0614, 0.2629, 1.6483, 0.6014, 1.8729, 0.5425, 0.0912, 0.0658, 0.1542]`. — [The Algorithm wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm); identical array in [ts-fsrs constant.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-spaced-repetition/ts-fsrs/main/packages/fsrs/src/constant.ts)

**Desired retention and workload**
- [DOC] Anki: desired retention default 90%; it "offers a good balance of retention and workload"; "Above 90% the workload increases very quickly, and above 97% the workload can be overwhelming." Lower values mean fewer reviews but more forgetting. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [DOC] "Values between 70% and 97% are considered reasonable." — [ABC of FSRS wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)
- [DOC] "The permissible range for desired retention is 0.70 to 0.97 (0.7 to 0.99 in Anki 23.10.1 or newer)"; 80–95% is reasonable and 90% suits most people; above 0.97 is discouraged; "Be conservative when adjusting this setting - higher values will greatly increase your workload"; the workload-vs-retention curve "depends on the user's parameters and learning habits". — [fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)
- [DOC] Anki source default `desired_retention: 0.9`, `historical_retention: 0.9`. — [anki rslib deckconfig/mod.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ankitects/anki/main/rslib/src/deckconfig/mod.rs)
- [DOC] Simulation claim: with FSRS users "have to do 20–30% fewer reviews than with SM-2 algorithm to achieve the same retention level" (wiki notes this comes from simulation; added 2026-02-12). — [ABC of FSRS wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)

**Compute minimum recommended retention (CMRR) and its replacement**
- [DOC] CMRR was an experimental tool that looked for the retention that "minimizes the ratio of the amount of time spent studying to the total knowledge acquired"; you could set retention higher than its output but "you shouldn't set your desired retention lower than recommended". From Anki 24.04 the only input was "Days to simulate". — [fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)
- [DOC] Exam-horizon hook: "If you are preparing for an exam that is 12 months away, set 'Days to simulate' to 365." (5 years suggested for language learning.) — [fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)
- [DOC] CMRR is removed as of Anki 25.07; replaced by "Help Me Decide (Experimental)" and the Simulator, which uses the user's real memory states and parameters with inputs Days to simulate, Additional new cards, New cards/day, Maximum reviews/day, Maximum interval. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [FORUM] The 25.07 announcement said CMRR was removed because it "doesn't give useful output for FSRS6" and may return in graph form (search snippet of the beta thread; not opened in full). — [Anki forum 25.06 beta thread](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/anki-25-06-beta/62271/167)

**Optimisation and usage rules**
- [DOC] Optimise parameters about once a month, or after every 2^n reviews (512, 1024, 2048 ...); no minimum review count from Anki 24.06 (400 in 24.04, 1000 before); the one habit FSRS cannot adapt to is "pressing 'Hard' instead of 'Again' when you forget". — [fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)
- [DOC] With FSRS keep (re)learning steps under one day (e.g. 10m or 30m), few in number; leaving steps empty lets FSRS control short-term scheduling (experimental). — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [DOC] "Reschedule cards on change" is off by default; turning it on can make many cards due at once. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [DOC] Maximum interval default is 100 years (`maximum_review_interval: 36_500`); lowering it increases workload. — [Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html); [anki source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ankitects/anki/main/rslib/src/deckconfig/mod.rs)

**Fuzz, load balancing, Easy Days**
- [DOC] Anki fuzz: no fuzz below 2.5 days; delta = 1.0 + 0.15 × (days in 2.5–7) + 0.10 × (days in 7–20) + 0.05 × (days above 20); bounds = round(interval ± delta). Test cases: 7 → [5, 9]; 17 → [14, 20]; 37 → [33, 41]. If a load-balancer context exists, `find_interval(interval, minimum, maximum)` is used; otherwise random fuzz. — [anki rslib scheduler/states/fuzz.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ankitects/anki/main/rslib/src/scheduler/states/fuzz.rs)
- [DOC] Anki 24.11: "Load balancing: within your fuzz range, Anki will now try to pick days that have fewer reviews waiting"; Easy Days: "you can now tell Anki to try avoid certain days of the week"; FSRS simulator added; "Let FSRS control short term schedule when no (re)learning steps are set" (experimental). — [Anki 24.11 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/ankitects/anki/release/24.11)
- [DOC] Easy Days adjusts each calculated interval slightly; setting every day to Reduced or Minimum gives the same workload as all Normal; changing it does not alter existing intervals. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [DOC] Helper add-on Easy Days: only review-stage cards affected; cards with intervals under 3 days are not rescheduled; also supports specific dates. — [fsrs4anki-helper README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper)

**Studying toward a fixed exam date**
- [DOC] Helper add-on **Advance**: decreases intervals of undue cards "based on current and requested R, and interval length"; stated use "When you want to review your material ahead of time, for example, before a test"; cards are chosen so "the deviation from the original review schedule is minimal". — [fsrs4anki-helper README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper)
- [DOC] The tutorial FAQ recommends Advance for seeing a deck sooner "because you have an exam coming up", since it "doesn't skew the grading history of the cards". — [fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)
- [DOC] Advance source code: candidates are review cards with `due > today`; sort key (ascending) = `1 - (R^(-1/decay) - 1) / (DR^(-1/decay) - 1)`, tie-break `-stability` (higher stability first); "safe" count = cards with key < 0.13; new interval = `today - last_review` (i.e. due today); card tagged `"v": "advance"`. — [fsrs4anki-helper advance.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper/main/schedule/advance.py)
- [DOC] Anki filtered decks: "Review ahead" shows cards due within N days; early reviews get "a new delay similar to what they would have received if you had reviewed them on time" on a sliding scale; "the 'review ahead' custom study setting is not appropriate for repeated use". With "Reschedule cards based on my answers" off, cards are shown in "preview mode" and return unchanged. Setting the card count above deck size includes every card "before a big test". — [Anki manual, Filtered Decks](https://docs.ankiweb.net/filtered-decks.html)
- [DOC] The Anki deck-options manual page has no exam or cramming section. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [FORUM, FSRS developer] Expertium (15 Apr 2024): "Set max. interval in a way that ensures that you will see each card at least once before the exam"; "Choose a high-but-not-crazy-high value of desired retention"; optionally use Advance. — [Anki forum, "How do I use FSRS for exam?"](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/how-do-i-use-fsrs-for-exam/43631)

### Inferences
- [COMPUTED] Interval as a multiple of stability, from `I(r,S)/S = (r^(-1/w20) - 1)/factor`. FSRS-6 default decay (w20 = 0.1542): r=0.70 → 9.29×; 0.75 → 5.57×; 0.80 → 3.32×; 0.85 → 1.91×; 0.90 → 1.00×; 0.92 → 0.73×; 0.95 → 0.40×; 0.97 → 0.22×; 0.99 → 0.07×. FSRS-4.5/5 fixed curve (decay 0.5): 0.70 → 4.44×; 0.80 → 2.40×; 0.85 → 1.64×; 0.95 → 0.46×; 0.97 → 0.27×; 0.99 → 0.09×. So moving a card from DR 0.90 to 0.95 shortens its next interval to roughly 40–46% of what it was, and 0.97 to roughly 22–27%; this is the mechanical basis of the manual's "workload increases very quickly" warning.
- [COMPUTED] Retrievability at multiples of S under FSRS-6 defaults: t = 2S → 0.846; 3S → 0.809; 5S → 0.761; 10S → 0.693 (FSRS-4.5: 0.825, 0.766, 0.678, 0.547). The FSRS-6 default curve is much flatter in the tail, so cards that are far overdue lose less predicted recall than under 4.5/5.
- Because `(R^(-1/decay) - 1)` is proportional to elapsed/S, the Advance sort key equals `1 - elapsed_days / optimal_interval_at_DR`: Advance picks the cards that are proportionally closest to being due (87%+ of the way through their interval counts as "safe"), not the cards with the lowest absolute R.
- An exam-aware planner built on ts-fsrs can stay inside documented FSRS behaviour by using only three levers per card: `request_retention`, `maximum_interval` (cap at days-to-exam so every card is seen at least once more), and an Advance-style pull-forward ordered by proportional closeness to due.
- For a known exam date E, the last review of a card must fall no earlier than `E - I(target_R, S)` for predicted R on exam day to be at least target_R. Cards whose S already satisfies `I(target_R, S) >= E - today` need no further review for the exam; others need one in that window. This follows directly from the forgetting-curve formula but is not something FSRS or Anki documents as a feature.
- Since ts-fsrs ships with `enable_fuzz = false` and has no load balancer, a planner that wants Anki-like day smoothing has to implement the fuzz-range search itself (ranges above).
- ts-fsrs is pinned to FSRS-6.0; the "current version" for implementation purposes is FSRS-6. FSRS-7 is worth tracking (large gain on same-day reviews) but is not yet a deployable dependency.

## 2. Backlog and missed days: review limits, postpone/advance, sort orders

### Takeaway
Documented practice is to cap daily reviews, pause new cards while a backlog exists, and work the backlog in an order chosen by retrievability; Anki's own simulations favour **descending retrievability** (least-forgotten first) for backlogs, while the older manual text recommends relative overdueness / ascending retrievability. The FSRS Helper's Postpone picks the cards that lose the least by waiting and stretches their interval by 5–10%.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Anki defaults: `new_per_day: 20`, `reviews_per_day: 200`, learn steps `[1.0, 10.0]` minutes, relearn steps `[10.0]`, `graduating_interval_good: 1`, `graduating_interval_easy: 4`, `leech_threshold: 8`, SM-2 `initial_ease: 2.5`, `easy_multiplier: 1.3`, `hard_multiplier: 1.2`, `review_order: Day` (due date), `new_mix: MixWithReviews`. — [anki rslib deckconfig/mod.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ankitects/anki/main/rslib/src/deckconfig/mod.rs)
- [DOC] Manual's sizing example: about 20 new cards a day leads to roughly 200 reviews a day; introduce fewer new cards if review load becomes too high. "New cards ignore review limit" is off by default; the manual advises pausing new cards while you have an overdue backlog. "Limits start from top" (off by default) applies parent-deck limits when studying a subdeck. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [DOC] Review sort orders: "Due date, then random" is the default, recommended "when you are up to date, or when you only have a small backlog"; relative overdueness (SM-2) shows cards most likely forgotten first and is "generally recommended if you have a large backlog that may take some time to get through"; with FSRS this is ascending retrievability, based on each card's retrievability and desired retention. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [DOC] Anki 24.11 release notes: "Decks can now be sorted by descending retrievability. Simulations have shown this is a better choice when you have a backlog", and the notes say this order is likely to become the default. — [Anki 24.11 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/ankitects/anki/release/24.11)
- [DOC] Filtered-deck catch-up recipe from the manual: `is:due prop:due>-7` (cards that became due in the past week) and `is:due prop:due<=-7` (older backlog), studied as separate decks. Filtered-deck "Relative overdueness" under FSRS "is calculated based on each card's retrievability" and the preset's desired retention. — [Anki manual, Filtered Decks](https://docs.ankiweb.net/filtered-decks.html)
- [DOC] Custom Study presets: increase today's new/review limit; review forgotten cards (answered Again within N days); review ahead; preview new cards; study by card state or tag. — [Anki manual, Filtered Decks](https://docs.ankiweb.net/filtered-decks.html)
- [DOC] Helper **Postpone**: increases intervals of cards due today "based on current and requested R, and interval length"; use "when you are dealing with a large number of reviews after taking a break from Anki or after rescheduling". — [fsrs4anki-helper README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper)
- [DOC] Postpone source: candidates `due <= today`, review queue; elapsed is approximated as `today - (due - ivl) + ivl*0.075`; sort key ascending = `(R^(-1/decay) - 1)/(DR^(-1/decay) - 1) - 1`, tie-break higher stability first; "safe" if key < 0.15; `delay = max(elapsed_days - ivl, 0)`; `new_ivl = min(max(1, ceil(ivl * (1.05 + 0.05*random)) + delay), max_ivl)` with RNG seeded by `cid + ivl`. — [fsrs4anki-helper postpone.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper/main/schedule/postpone.py)
- [DOC] Helper **Schedule a Break**: spreads cards due during a break across follow-up days and "uses a cost function to minimize deviation from original due dates"; warns it reduces future retention. **Flatten**: sets a max reviews/day for future days; "will find out the days that exceed the limit and postpone the extra cards to the future". **Disperse Siblings** spreads cards of the same note. — [fsrs4anki-helper README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper)
- [FORUM] Reddit advice: to clear a backlog use Descending Retrievability and "do as much as you can reasonably maintain without burning out"; easy cards come first so the remaining backlog gets harder. — [Reddit mirror, r/Anki](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/Anki/comments/1k2xolm/need_some_help_with_anki)
- [DOC] SuperMemo: Postpone is for overload; Mercy redistributes outstanding material over a chosen period; since SuperMemo 2006 auto-postpone needs only element priorities, and "delays for top priority material are minimized". Postpone skips items with forgetting index < 6, topics with A-Factor < 1.01, elements postponed > 250 times, and items with priority above 3.5% (values from archived help; may differ in current versions). — [SuperMemo archived help: Postpone](https://www-v1.supermemo.com/archives1990-2015/help/postpone); [supermemo.guru Auto-postpone](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Auto-postpone); [SuperMemo archived help: Postpone/Advance/Mercy](https://www-v1.supermemo.com/archives1990-2015/help/pam)
- [DOC] Reddy et al. (KDD 2016) model the Leitner system as a queueing network and predict "a sharp phase transition" in learning outcomes as the rate of new-item introduction increases; tested with a Mechanical Turk experiment. — [arXiv:1602.07032](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07032)

### Inferences
- The Postpone key is `elapsed'/optimal_interval - 1` (relative overdueness after a simulated 7.5% slip). Sorting ascending means the cards postponed first are those that are proportionally least overdue, with long-stability cards preferred; the cards actually reviewed are therefore the proportionally most overdue.
- Two distinct backlog objectives lead to opposite orders: (a) "minimise forgetting among cards I will get to" → descending retrievability (rescue cards that are still recallable before they lapse; Anki's simulation result); (b) "find what I have already forgotten" → ascending retrievability. For an exam with limited days, (a) fits the weeks before, and a final-pass list of low-R cards fits the last days.
- A planner with a fixed daily time budget should treat new-item intake as the control variable: Reddy et al.'s phase transition and Anki's "pause new cards during backlog" both say review debt becomes unrecoverable above a threshold intake rate. The Anki 20-new/200-review default implies roughly a 1:10 new-to-review card ratio at steady state for SM-2-era defaults.
- Postponing by a small multiplicative stretch (5–10% of the interval plus existing delay) keeps the predicted R drop bounded; the same rule can be used to spill excess reviews to tomorrow when a day's budget is exceeded.

### Gaps
- The simulation behind the 24.11 "descending retrievability is better for backlogs" claim (metric, assumptions) was not located; only the release-note sentence is cited.
- The current Anki manual wording for all retrievability-based sort orders was only partially captured (the fetched page text described ascending retrievability but not descending).
- Reddy et al. 2016 abstract gives no closed-form threshold; the paper's formula for the maximum sustainable arrival rate was not retrieved.

## 3. SuperMemo: forgetting index, Plan, postpone, and Wozniak on exam preparation

### Takeaway
SuperMemo's equivalent of desired retention is the forgetting index (default 10%, range 3–20%, recommended 8–13%), which maps to average retention by `-FI/ln(1-FI)`. SuperMemo's own position is that spaced repetition is "nearly useless" for knowledge needed for less than a week; its transferable planning idea is Plan's proportional compression of activities into available time and priority-driven auto-postpone.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] "Forgetting index is the proportion of elements that are not remembered during repetitions"; default 10%; "SuperMemo makes it possible to choose a forgetting index in the range from 3% to 20%"; "In most cases, the value of 8%-13% will work best"; "The higher the forgetting index, the faster you learn, but the less you remember!" — [supermemo.guru, Forgetting index in SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting_index_in_SuperMemo)
- [DOC] Average retention formula: `retention = -FI / ln(1 - FI)`; table: FI 3% → 98.49%; 5% → 97.47%; 10% → 94.91%; 15% → 92.29%; 20% → 89.62%. The page rejects the linear approximation `1 - 0.5*FI` because "forgetting is approximately exponential in nature". — [supermemo.guru, Forgetting index in SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting_index_in_SuperMemo)
- [DOC] Tuning rule: if you remember too little, reduce FI by one percentage point; if you repeat too often, increase it. FI can be set per element; measured FI is usually higher than requested, especially below 5%, due to interval limits, delays, Postpone use and low-priority items in an overloaded collection. — [supermemo.guru, Forgetting index in SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting_index_in_SuperMemo)
- [DOC] "SuperMemo is nearly useless for cramming knowledge that is supposed to last less than a week." "For fast cramming for an exam, use traditional review, recall, repeat approach known to crammers for ages." "The power of SuperMemo increases in proportion to the expected lifetime of knowledge in your memory." "If you have all your knowledge stored in a computer file (e.g. HTML), cramming is possible with subset review." — [supermemo.guru, Myth: SuperMemo is a great tool for cramming](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Myth:_SuperMemo_is_a_great_tool_for_cramming)
- [DOC] Final drill (re-drilling items failed today) can be randomised or skipped, and "must always be considered of lower priority than reviewing the outstanding material". — [SuperMemo help, Learning options](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/learning-tab-in-options)
- [DOC] Plan: "You produce a list of activities and the desired time allocations, and SuperMemo checks the available time and shortens all activities in proportion"; its main purpose is "to keep optimum proportions of time devoted to particular activities". — [SuperMemo archived help: Schedule manager (Plan)](https://www-v1.supermemo.com/archives1990-2015/help/plan)
- [DOC] "Plan is a scheduling program integrated with SuperMemo for Windows. It makes it possible to organize a productive day with minimum stress"; it anchors sleep, meals and exercise in the circadian cycle. — [supermemo.guru, Plan](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Plan)
- [DOC] Overload handling: priority queue + auto-sort + auto-postpone (SuperMemo 2006+) replaced Mercy; the user only assigns priorities and lower-priority reviews are delayed first. — [supermemo.guru, Auto-postpone](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Auto-postpone); [SuperMemo archived help](https://www-v1.supermemo.com/archives1990-2015/help/pam)
- [DOC] Wozniak-derived "SuperTip": cramming thousands of items before an exam "is possible, but completely ineffective in the long run"; understand before memorising (from the Twenty Rules). — [SuperMemo blog, SuperTip #1](https://www.supermemo.com/blog/try-to-understand-it-first-supertip-1)

### Inferences
- [COMPUTED] The same exponential-forgetting identity gives average retention between reviews for any target: FI 10% → ~94.9%. RemNote quotes the same rule of thumb for FSRS-style schedulers (90% target → about 95% actual; see section 4). A planner that reports "expected recall today across the course" should therefore expect a value above the desired-retention setting when the learner is up to date.
- SuperMemo's stance implies a horizon rule: if the exam is under about a week away and nothing needs to be retained afterwards, a spaced scheduler adds little over repeated pass-through with retesting of failures; spaced scheduling earns its cost when the retention horizon is weeks or longer.
- Plan's proportional-shortening rule is a simple, explainable way to fit a day's planned blocks into the time actually available: scale every flexible block by `available / requested`, keep fixed blocks fixed.
- Priority-based auto-postpone is the SuperMemo analogue of Helper Postpone: when over budget, defer by declared priority first, by memory risk second.

### Gaps
- help.supermemo.org returned HTTP 403, so the current Plan help page (fixed/floating/rigid slots, delay handling, statistics) and the Tasklist manager page (priority = value/time) are not quoted.
- I found no SuperMemo or Wozniak text giving a concrete exam-preparation procedure with numbers (e.g. lowering FI N weeks before an exam); the guru page on FI gives no exam advice.
- Incremental reading was not researched beyond the search-level mention; nothing citable was collected on its scheduling parameters (A-Factor defaults).

## 4. Exam-oriented schedulers: research and products that optimise for a deadline

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed deadline-aware work is thin but consistent: personalised, model-driven review beat fixed spaced and massed schedules on a cumulative exam (Lindsey et al.), and optimal-control formulations reduce to "review with intensity proportional to forgetting probability" (MEMORIZE) or "greedily pick the item with the largest marginal gain in recall over the horizon" (Hunziker et al.). Among products, RemNote's Exam Scheduler is the best-documented design: a normal scheduler plus a learning period, a final review pass of every card, a catch-up period, and an exam-specific daily goal.

### Cited Findings

**Research**
- [DOC] Lindsey, Shroyer, Pashler & Mozer (2014), Psychological Science 25(3), 639–647, doi:10.1177/0956797613504302: personalised review was "integrated into a semester-long middle-school foreign-language course via retrieval-practice software"; on a cumulative exam after the semester, "personalized review yielded a 16.5% boost in course retention over current educational practice" (massed study) and a 10.0% improvement over a one-size-fits-all spaced schedule, under matched review time. — [APS journal page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797613504302/)
- [DOC] Same study, secondary summaries: 179 eighth-grade Spanish students; software used about 30 minutes per week; retention test about a month after the semester; personalised review helped most for material from the first chapters. — [ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/01/140121093046.htm); [author PDF](https://home.cs.colorado.edu/~mozer/Research/Selected%20Publications/reprints/LindseyShroyerPashlerMozer2014.pdf)
- [DOC] Tabibian et al. (2019), PNAS 116(10), 3988–3993, doi:10.1073/pnas.1815156116 (MEMORIZE). Model: `m(t) = exp(-n(t) * (t - t_r))`; forgetting rate n is multiplied by (1 - alpha) after a successful recall and (1 + beta) after a failure. Loss: `½(1 - m)² + ½ q u²`, where q "trades off recall probability and number of item reviews". Optimal reviewing intensity: `u*(t) = q^(-1/2) * (1 - m(t))`; "the optimal reviewing schedule is given by the recall probability itself". Review times are sampled from an inhomogeneous Poisson process by thinning. — [PMC6410796](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6410796/)
- [DOC] MEMORIZE evaluation: Duolingo data, ~12 million sessions, ~5.2 million (user, word) pairs over 2 weeks; a natural experiment (not randomised) comparing users whose actual reviews resembled MEMORIZE vs. uniform or threshold schedules; MEMORIZE-like learners showed lower empirical forgetting rates. — [PMC6410796](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6410796/)
- [DOC] Hunziker et al. (NeurIPS 2019), "Teaching Multiple Concepts to a Forgetful Learner": frames teaching multiple concepts in limited time as discrete optimisation with performance guarantees, generic across memory models and interactive; validated in simulation and two user studies (vocabulary; animal species recognition) against "popular heuristic approaches". — [arXiv:1805.08322](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08322)
- [DOC] Ye, Su & Cao (KDD 2022), "A Stochastic Shortest Path Algorithm for Optimizing Spaced Repetition Scheduling", pp. 4381–4390, doi:10.1145/3534678.3539081: SSP-MMC is the scheduler used in MaiMemo; repo includes a DHP memory model and 220 million memory-behaviour logs. FSRS "is based on MaiMemo's papers at ACM KDD and IEEE TKDE". — [maimemo/SSP-MMC](https://github.com/maimemo/SSP-MMC); [awesome-fsrs wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm)
- [DOC] Reddy, Levine & Dragan (2017), "Accelerating Human Learning with Deep Reinforcement Learning": formulates review scheduling as deep RL trained against simulated students because training on real students from scratch is infeasible; code repo "Train and evaluate review scheduling algorithms for spaced repetition". Authors call for user studies with real students. — [rddy/deeptutor](https://github.com/rddy/deeptutor)

**RemNote Exam Scheduler**
- [DOC] Rationale: standard spaced repetition maximises long-run retention; for exams the goal is remembering a set of material as well as possible at one moment. Retrievability is "the probability you'll be able to successfully recall the answer within a short amount of time"; most systems aim for 85–90% desired retention; rule of thumb: a 90% target yields about 95% actual average retrievability. — [RemNote: Understanding the Exam Scheduler](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9102040-understanding-the-exam-scheduler)
- [DOC] It complements the user's chosen scheduler (Anki SM-2 or FSRS named), which "does most of the work", and adds: **Learning Period** (a new card is repeated until recalled successfully twice before spacing starts); **Final Review Period** ("one last review of every card just before your exam"); **Ensure Mastery** ("Add extra practice for shaky cards": a missed card returns sooner and needs two correct in a row); **Catch Up Period** (proposes a temporary increase to the daily goal rather than silently rescheduling); **Exam Daily Goal** computed from practice due this week and total practice remaining. — [RemNote: Understanding the Exam Scheduler](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9102040-understanding-the-exam-scheduler)
- [DOC] Setup flow: choose material; exam date; expected new cards per week (10/25/50/100/custom); spread unpracticed cards before the exam (recommended) or mark all due now; toggle "Review Every Card One Final Time"; confirm daily goal (default "Practice Everything Due", sample max 50 cards/day); deselect non-study weekdays (due cards are spread over remaining days); toggle Ensure Mastery; advanced: choose scheduler, max cards/day, repetitions per card, start date. Final review happens "a couple of days before the exam". Exam cards are prioritised first in the global queue. Small daily-goal changes are applied automatically; large changes prompt the user. — [RemNote: Preparing for an Exam](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9101991-preparing-for-an-exam)
- [DOC] Plan presentation: RemNote "calculates how much practice you need between now and exam day" and spreads it across remaining weeks; views: today's goal + progress chart, weekly (cards to learn and reviews due per week by document), monthly calendar ending at a flagged Exam Day, and a graph split into "Learning Period" and "Final Review"; a banner flags content "not yet in your schedule". — [RemNote: Exam Study Plan](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/16081995-exam-study-plan)

**Other products**
- [DOC] Anki: no exam mode; filtered decks ("Review ahead", study everything by setting the count above deck size "before a big test") and the Helper's Advance are the documented tools (section 1). — [Anki manual, Filtered Decks](https://docs.ankiweb.net/filtered-decks.html)
- [DOC] Quizlet (2017 launch): the user enters a test date and "Quizlet Learn will generate a study session plan leading up to the exam", with reminders and progress monitoring. — [EdSurge, 9 Mar 2017](https://edsurge.com/news/2017-03-09-quizlet-adds-new-study-plan-feature-to-help-students-study-smarter)
- [DOC] Quizlet engineering (2017): Learn ordered terms with a multiple logistic regression on answer history and elapsed time, later replaced by a "Recurrent Power Law" memory model (search snippet; article returned HTTP 403). — [Quizlet tech blog](https://medium.com/tech-quizlet/improving-learn-ing-quizlets-new-memory-model-3c2271e5eaf4)
- [DOC, secondary] Quizlet 2023: a personalised "Memory Score" and recommended review times. — [Educators Technology, Oct 2023](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/10/quizlets-latest-back-to-school-features.html)
- [DOC] Brainscape: users rate confidence 1–5 per card, which "determines how frequently to repeat the flashcard" (1 = will forget in minutes, 5 = will never forget). — [Wikipedia: Brainscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainscape); [Brainscape Academy](https://brainscape.com/academy/science-never-forgetting-spaced-repetition)
- [DOC] Mochi: own default algorithm that "adjusts each card's interval up or down by fixed multipliers" on Remembered/Forgot (two grades); FSRS is optional; forgetting sends the card to a re-review queue, and forgetting again there resets it as new. — [Mochi docs: FSRS](https://mochi.cards/docs/reviewing/fsrs/); [Mochi docs (old overview)](https://mochi.cards/docs/reviewing/index_old)

**Medical-student Anki use before USMLE (observational)**
- [DOC] Deng, Gluckstein & Larsen (2015), Perspectives on Medical Education: survey of 72 students; in multivariate regression, "each additional 445 boards-style practice questions or 1700 unique Anki flashcards was associated with an additional point on Step 1"; unique Firecracker flashcards did not predict Step 1. — [PubMed 26498443](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26498443/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40037-015-0220-x)
- [DOC] Wothe et al. (2023), Int. J. Medical Students: 165 students; daily Anki use correlated with higher Step 1 (P = .039) but not Step 2. — [IJMS](https://ijms.pitt.edu/IJMS/article/view/1549)
- [DOC, search-level] Lu et al. (2021): Anki use associated with higher Step 1 scores, more consistent use with higher scores (cross-sectional, self-report). A single-school cohort comparison (35 Anki vs 268 non-Anki students) found 223.71 vs 222.58 with no significant correlation. A 2026 systematic review of 11 studies notes most are observational and self-reported. — [PMC8651966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8651966); [PMC10403443](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10403443); [Med Sci Educ 2026 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40670-026-02643-5) (URL-to-study mapping not individually verified)

### Inferences
- A deadline-aware layer over FSRS can copy RemNote's structure without changing the memory model: (1) intake pacing: spread unseen items evenly over study days up to a cut-off before the exam; (2) learning gate: two successful recalls before an item counts as learned; (3) normal FSRS spacing in between; (4) final review pass of every item in the last days; (5) explicit catch-up proposals when behind; (6) an exam-specific daily goal derived from remaining work divided by remaining study days.
- MEMORIZE's result gives a principled priority score when reviews are budget-limited: review propensity proportional to `1 - R(now)`. For a deadline, the analogous greedy rule (Hunziker et al.'s framing) is to pick the item whose review now yields the largest increase in predicted recall at the exam date; with FSRS this is computable as `R_exam(after review now) - R_exam(no review)` using the stability-update formulas.
- RemNote's "final review of every card" and the FSRS developer's "max interval so each card is seen at least once before the exam" are the same safeguard against model error on long-interval cards; both ignore predicted R and trade efficiency for certainty.
- The evidence for exam benefit from flashcard volume in medical students is correlational; it supports "coverage and consistency matter", not any specific scheduler setting.

### Gaps
- Lindsey et al.'s full method details (the DASH model, how the personalised scheduler chose items, per-chapter effect sizes, exact follow-up interval) were not retrieved from the paper itself; the author PDF failed a TLS check. The "DASH" name is attested only via the benchmark repo's model list, not the abstract.
- Hunziker et al.'s exact greedy rule, guarantees and user-study effect sizes are not in the arXiv abstract and were not retrieved.
- Ye et al. KDD 2022: abstract, cost function, Bellman equation and headline improvement percentages were not retrieved (paper page returned empty content). The TKDE 2023 paper was not retrieved either.
- Reddy et al. 2017 results (which RL policy vs which baselines) were not retrieved.
- RemNote does not publish the length rule for the Final Review Period, an exam-day target retention, or what happens to cards after the exam date.
- No citable primary documentation was found for Quizlet's current per-card scheduling, Brainscape's interval table per confidence level, or Mochi's default multiplier values; a third-party blog disputes that Quizlet computes per-card due dates at all ([Notelyn, June 2026](https://www.notelyn.com/blog/quizlet-spaced-repetition)).
- No controlled study was found comparing an exam-date-aware scheduler against a plain FSRS/SM-2 schedule on exam scores.

## 5. Duolingo half-life regression (Settles & Meeder 2016)

### Takeaway
HLR models recall as `p = 2^(-Δ/h)` with half-life `h = 2^(Θ·x)` learned from features of practice history; it cut recall-prediction error roughly in half versus Duolingo's Leitner system, but the product gains came from engagement metrics and a simpler feature set, and on the modern Anki benchmark HLR is far less accurate than FSRS. The durable lessons are: fit the forgetting model to logged data, regularise features, and validate by A/B test on behaviour rather than on prediction error alone.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Citation: Settles, B. & Meeder, B. (2016). "A Trainable Spaced Repetition Model for Language Learning." Proc. ACL 2016, pp. 1848–1858, doi:10.18653/v1/P16-1174. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174/)
- [DOC] Abstract claims: HLR reduces "error by 45%+ compared to several baselines at predicting student recall rates" and "was able to improve Duolingo daily student engagement by 12% in an operational user study". — [paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] Model: `p = 2^(-Δ/h)` (Δ = lag since last practice, h = half-life); `ĥ_Θ = 2^(Θ·x)`; at Δ = h, p = 0.5. Loss: `(p - p̂)² + α(h - ĥ)² + λ‖Θ‖²`. Features: counts of prior correct and incorrect exposures (square-root transformed in practice) plus sparse lexeme-tag indicators. — [paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] Pimsleur and Leitner are special cases: Leitner corresponds to Θ = {x⊕: 1, x⊖: -1} (each correct answer doubles the interval, each incorrect halves it); Pimsleur's schedule (5 s, 25 s, 2 min, 10 min, 1 h, 5 h, 1 d, 5 d, 25 d, 4 months, 2 years) corresponds to Θ = {x_n: 2.4, x_b: -16.5}. — [paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] Historical evaluation on two weeks of logs, 12.9 million student-word session traces (MAE / AUC / half-life correlation): HLR 0.128 / 0.538 / 0.201; HLR without lexeme features 0.128 / 0.537 / 0.160; Leitner 0.235 / 0.542 / -0.098; Pimsleur 0.445 / 0.510 / -0.132; logistic regression 0.211 / 0.513; constant prediction p̄ = 0.859 gives MAE 0.175. Dropping the half-life term from the loss more than doubles MAE (0.350). — [paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] Use in product: strength meters "represent the average probability that the student can, at any moment, correctly recall a random target word from the lessons in this skill"; practice sessions draw exercises indexed with words "due for practice according to student model" and target the weakest words. At launch Duolingo used a Leitner variant; students complained the Leitner-based meters "did not adequately reflect what they had learned". — [paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] A/B Experiment I (HLR vs Leitner, six weeks, just under 1 million students), change in daily retention: any activity +0.3%, new lessons +0.3%, practice sessions -7.3% (significant). Experiment II (HLR without lexeme features vs full HLR, two weeks, 3.3 million students): any activity +12.0%, lessons +1.7%, practice +9.5% (all significant). The full-feature model had overfit lexeme weights, making some words "decay rapidly, regardless of how often they practiced". — [paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] Public data and code: ~13 million learning traces (columns `p_recall, timestamp, delta, user_id, learning_language, ui_language, lexeme_id, lexeme_string, history_seen, history_correct, session_seen, session_correct`); MIT licence. — [duolingo/halflife-regression](https://github.com/duolingo/halflife-regression)
- [DOC] On the Anki benchmark HLR (3 params) scores log loss 0.4694, RMSE(bins) 0.1275, AUC 0.6369, worse on log loss and RMSE than the constant-average baseline (0.3945, 0.1034) and far behind FSRS-6 (0.3460, 0.0653, 0.7034). — [srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)

### Inferences
- The headline "12%" came from simplifying the model (removing lexeme features), not from HLR replacing Leitner; the first experiment showed essentially no engagement gain and a drop in practice. Better prediction error did not by itself improve behaviour.
- The reported AUC values (~0.51–0.54) show that all 2016-era models barely ranked recall outcomes; calibration (MAE) drove the strength meters. For a planner showing mastery/strength bars, calibration of the displayed number matters more to users than ranking.
- A displayed strength that decays visibly drives users to "keep the tree gold" by practising instead of learning new material; a planner should make sure decay-driven review prompts target what the learner actually needs, or they crowd out new learning.
- HLR's exponential curve and count-only state are superseded by DSR models with a power-law curve; there is no reason to adopt HLR over FSRS for item-level memory, but the half-life framing (`h` where p = 0.5) is an easy way to explain strength to users.

### Gaps
- Duolingo has not published (that I found) an evaluation of HLR on learning outcomes (test scores), only on engagement.
- Whether HLR is still in production at Duolingo in 2026 was not established.

## 6. Mastery / knowledge-tracing models for choosing what to practise next

### Takeaway
The standard decision rules are simple and well documented: BKT declares mastery when P(known) reaches about 0.95; PFA is a logistic model on per-skill success and failure counts; Elo updates learner and item ratings online with a decaying step size (a = 1, b = 0.05) and a target success rate (about 75%); products add retention checks on top (ALEKS knowledge checks every ~20 topics/5–10 hours/60 days, Khan mastery challenges, Math Academy implicit repetitions through an encompassing graph).

### Cited Findings

**Bayesian Knowledge Tracing**
- [DOC] Four parameters per skill: p(L0) prior knowledge, p(T) learning per opportunity, p(S) slip, p(G) guess. Updates: after correct, `P(L|correct) = P(L)(1-S) / [P(L)(1-S) + (1-P(L))G]`; after wrong, `P(L|wrong) = P(L)S / [P(L)S + (1-P(L))(1-G)]`; then `P(L_next) = P(L|obs) + (1 - P(L|obs))·T`; predicted correctness `P(C) = P(L)(1-S) + (1-P(L))G`. Original reference: Corbett & Anderson (1995), UMUAI 4(4), 253–278, doi:10.1007/BF01099821. — [Wikipedia: Bayesian knowledge tracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_knowledge_tracing)
- [DOC] Mastery criterion: "mastery is assumed when the knowledge estimate (i.e., P(Ln)) exceeds 0.95" (EDM 2025 short paper describing the Corbett & Anderson convention). — [EDM 2025 short paper](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.short-papers.4/2025.EDM.short-papers.4.pdf)
- [DOC] Pavlik et al. describe existing tutors as algorithms "which simply schedule practice until 95% mastery of the KC"; their KT fits bounded initial-knowledge and transition probabilities at maximums of 0.85 and 0.3. — [Pavlik, Cen & Koedinger 2009 (ERIC ED506305)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED506305.pdf)
- [DOC] The standard BKT transition has no forgetting term (only the +T learning term appears in the update). — [Wikipedia: Bayesian knowledge tracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_knowledge_tracing)

**Performance Factors Analysis**
- [DOC] Pavlik, Cen & Koedinger (2009), AIED: `m(i, j∈KCs, s, f) = Σ_j (β_j + γ_j·s_ij + ρ_j·f_ij)`, `p(m) = 1/(1 + e^(-m))`; β = KC easiness, s = prior successes, f = prior failures for student i on KC j, γ and ρ scale their effect; multi-KC items sum the components (compensatory). No per-student parameter, so it can be used adaptively. Across 4 datasets "the PFA model has better LL, BIC, r and A'" than KT, "while the differences are not large". γ was bounded at a minimum of 0. — [ERIC ED506305](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED506305.pdf)

**Elo-based ratings**
- [DOC] Pelánek (2016), Computers & Education, doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2016.03.017: `P(correct) = 1/(1 + e^(-(θ_s - d_i)))`; `θ_s := θ_s + K·(correct - P)`; `d_i := d_i + K·(P - correct)`. For a k-option multiple-choice item: `P = 1/k + (1 - 1/k)/(1 + e^(-(θ_s - d_i)))`. Constant K = 0.4 was used in earlier work; preferred is an uncertainty function `U(n) = a/(1 + b·n)` with n = number of prior answers and grid-searched values a = 1, b = 0.05, which "provides a good starting point". New or changed items are reset to difficulty 0. — [Pelánek 2016 accepted manuscript](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)
- [DOC] Question selection in the Outline Maps system: "uses a target success rate (e.g., 75 %) and adaptively selects questions in such a way that students' achieved performance is close to this target". More complex Elo variants "can improve performance, but the improvement is often small". — [Pelánek 2016 accepted manuscript](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)
- [DOC] Duolingo Birdbrain (Bicknell & Brust, 7 Oct 2020) continuously estimates "both how much our learners know as well as the difficulty level" of exercises and predicts the probability of a correct answer; the Session Generator uses this to pick exercises at the right difficulty; personalising "over 20% of lessons" by Oct 2020. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain/)

**ALEKS**
- [DOC] A new Knowledge Check is triggered "After 20 topics are learned AND 5 hours are spent in learning mode", or "After 10 hours are spent in learning mode", or "After 60 days without a Knowledge Check" (30 days for Higher Education Chemistry); it may also occur after a module due date. Topics not retained (wrong answer or "I Don't Know") "may move from the Mastered or Learned category to the Remaining category" and return to the learning path. — [McGraw Hill ALEKS support](https://www.mheducation.com/support/aleks-support-center/knowledge/why-does-aleks-periodically-give-me-new-knowledge-checks.html)

**Khan Academy**
- [DOC] Levels and mastery points (100 per skill): Attempted (< 70% on an exercise), Familiar (70%+; 50 points), Proficient (100% on an exercise while Familiar; 80 points), Mastered (all questions on the skill correct on a Unit Test or Course Challenge while Proficient; 100 points). Missing questions on a mixed-skill assessment can demote a level. — [Khan Academy Help: Mastery levels](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853); [Khan Academy Help: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631)
- [DOC] Mastery Challenges (spaced review; Math only): unlocked after reaching Familiar on at least 3 skills and Proficient on at least 1, and more than 12 hours since the last one started; always 6 questions covering 3 skills (2 each); both correct → level up, both wrong → level down, split → unchanged; 12 hours to complete; one per 12-hour period. — [Khan Academy Help: Mastery Challenges](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360037494231)

**Math Academy (FIRe: Fractional Implicit Repetition)**
- [DOC] Each answer is a fractional repetition that flows through an "encompassing" graph (distinct from the prerequisite graph). Passing an advanced topic gives implicit credit to the simpler topics it encompasses, several layers deep; failing a simpler topic penalises the advanced topics that encompass it. Encompassing weights are the fraction of the simpler topic exercised (full = 1; partial weights make credit "fade off"); unspecified weights are assumed zero. "The specific implementation is proprietary." — [Justin Skycak, "Optimized, Individualized Spaced Repetition in Hierarchical Knowledge Structures"](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)
- [DOC] Model structure given in the article: `repNum → max(0, repNum + speed · decay^failed · rawDelta)`; `memory → max(0, memory + rawDelta) · (0.5)^(days / interval)`. rawDelta is positive on pass and negative on fail and is discounted when the review is early (memory still high); `decay` starts at 1 and grows the more overdue a review is; `speed` is a per-student-per-topic ratio (ability speed-up over topic difficulty slow-down): 2× speed counts a review as 2 repetitions, 0.5× as half. When speed is below 1, the topic discards implicit credit and forces explicit reviews. — [justinmath.com](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)
- [DOC] "Repetition compression": when reviews are due the system selects reviews and new lessons whose implicit repetitions "knock out" other due reviews "like dominoes"; estimate without compression: 3 new topics/day with 4-question reviews leads to about 60 review questions/day. Lessons have about 10 questions; reviews 3–5. — [justinmath.com](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)
- [DOC] Lessons: move on after two practice problems correct in a row, up to 5 problems per knowledge point; students advance only after demonstrating mastery of the previous knowledge point; an adaptive diagnostic (30–45 minutes) places the student at their knowledge frontier. Returning students are recommended a fresh diagnostic. BKT is mentioned only as an early model that was later replaced. — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://mathacademy.com/how-it-works); [justinmath.com](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)

### Inferences
- BKT and PFA have no forgetting; FSRS has no notion of "learned vs not yet learned". A planner with both per-concept mastery and FSRS can use a mastery model to gate entry (e.g. P(known) ≥ 0.95, or Khan-style Proficient) and FSRS to schedule retention afterwards, with a failed retention check demoting mastery (ALEKS and Khan both demote on missed mixed review).
- Khan's 2-question-per-skill mixed challenge is a cheap retention probe: 2/2 promotes, 0/2 demotes, 1/2 no change. It is robust to slips and guesses in the same way BKT's S and G are, without fitting parameters.
- With practice questions tagged to several concepts, PFA's compensatory sum and Math Academy's encompassing weights are two ways to credit multiple concepts from one answer. Giving fractional FSRS-style review credit to prerequisite concepts when a harder question is answered correctly reduces explicit review load; Math Academy's safeguards are worth copying: discount credit for early reviews, do not grant implicit credit to concepts the learner finds hard (speed < 1), and propagate failures to dependants.
- Elo with `K = 1/(1 + 0.05n)` gives a parameter-free cold start for question difficulty and learner ability, and the guess-corrected formula matters for multiple-choice items. A 75% target success rate (Outline Maps) is a documented operating point for selecting question difficulty; Duolingo's reported "Goldilocks" target is in the same range (search-level: about 70% for new material, per [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-duolingo-uses-ai-in-every-part-of-its-app), not verified in the primary blog).
- ALEKS's triggers combine a work-based and a time-based condition; a planner can use the same pattern for cumulative checks: after N concepts learned and H hours, or D days, whichever comes first, and before each known quiz/exam date.

### Gaps
- Corbett & Anderson (1995) was not read directly; the 0.95 threshold is cited via later papers. Typical fitted BKT parameter values were not collected.
- Knowledge-space-theory internals of ALEKS (how the knowledge state is updated, number of questions per check) are not public in the page retrieved.
- Math Academy's actual weights, interval table, and task-selection objective are proprietary; "The Math Academy Way" book was not opened. The Khan support pages returned 403 on direct fetch, so the Khan rules are taken from search-result extracts; two Khan pages state the Familiar band differently (70–85% vs 70–99%).
- Birdbrain's model form (IRT/Elo-like) and target probability are not stated in Duolingo's own blog post; they come from secondary reports.
- Deep knowledge tracing and newer KT models were out of scope and not researched.

## 7. Session sizing, prioritisation among due reviews / weak skills / new material, and plan presentation

### Takeaway
Products size sessions either by card-count limits (Anki: 20 new / 200 reviews per day) or by time-equivalent points (Math Academy: 1 XP ≈ 1 minute, 30–60 XP/day sustainable), put due reviews and exam material ahead of new material, pause or throttle new items when behind, and present the plan as a daily goal plus a calendar or graph ending at the exam.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Anki: defaults 20 new/day and 200 reviews/day; new cards mixed with reviews by default; default review order is by due date then random; manual's example ratio is ~20 new → ~200 reviews; pause new cards during a backlog; per-day limit overrides via Custom Study. — [anki source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ankitects/anki/main/rslib/src/deckconfig/mod.rs); [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html); [Anki manual, Filtered Decks](https://docs.ankiweb.net/filtered-decks.html)
- [DOC] Anki simulator inputs for workload planning: days to simulate, additional new cards, new cards/day, maximum reviews/day, maximum interval, driven by the user's real memory states. — [Anki manual, Deck Options](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)
- [DOC] Math Academy: "each XP being roughly equivalent to 1-minute of focused effort"; XP awarded per completed task depends on performance (bonus for perfect scores); adjustable daily XP goal; diagnostic report gives estimated completion dates at several daily XP goals; a timed quiz becomes available every 150 XP covering recent topics, with missed topics immediately assigned as reviews; students choose from a set of algorithm-selected tasks. — [Math Academy: How It Works](https://mathacademy.com/how-it-works)
- [DOC] Math Academy sustainable load: "the maximum sustainable XP rate is around 50-60 XP/day"; 30 XP/day still gives fast progress (search-level extract of the author's blog). A third-party reviewer reports 1 XP taking 2–3 minutes for their family. — [justinmath.com, highest sustainable daily XP](https://justinmath.com/what-is-the-highest-sustainable-daily-xp-on-math-academy/); [Well-Trained Mind forum review](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/733445-my-ongoing-review-of-mathacademycom-updated-62824)
- [DOC] Math Academy prioritisation: clear due reviews using tasks (including new lessons) that implicitly knock out the most due reviews; once reviews are cleared, choose tasks that fend off future explicit reviews; climb the knowledge graph evenly to keep options open. — [justinmath.com](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)
- [DOC] RemNote: exam cards go first in the global queue, ahead of "Currently Studying"; daily goal default "Practice Everything Due" with a max-cards/day cap (sample 50); non-study weekdays can be excluded; new cards per week estimated at setup; unpracticed cards spread before the exam; Catch Up proposes a temporary daily-goal increase; small goal changes automatic, large ones need confirmation; plan shown as today's goal, progress chart, weekly and monthly views, and a Learning Period / Final Review graph. — [RemNote: Preparing for an Exam](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9101991-preparing-for-an-exam); [RemNote: Exam Study Plan](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/16081995-exam-study-plan); [RemNote: Understanding the Exam Scheduler](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9102040-understanding-the-exam-scheduler)
- [DOC] Khan Academy: review is a fixed-size unit (6 questions, 3 skills) at most once per 12 hours; course progress shown as mastery points out of 100 per skill. — [Khan Academy Help: Mastery Challenges](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360037494231)
- [DOC] Duolingo (2016): strength meters per skill (four bars = "golden"); practice sessions target the weakest words; under Leitner, students practised "just to keep the tree gold" instead of learning new material. — [Settles & Meeder 2016](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] Lindsey et al. classroom deployment: about 30 minutes per week of retrieval-practice software, review time matched across conditions. — [ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/01/140121093046.htm)
- [DOC] SuperMemo Plan: activities with desired durations are shortened proportionally to fit available time. — [SuperMemo archived help: Plan](https://www-v1.supermemo.com/archives1990-2015/help/plan)
- [DOC] Helper Flatten caps future days at a max reviews/day and pushes the excess later; Easy Days reduces load on chosen weekdays or specific dates. — [fsrs4anki-helper README](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki-helper)
- [DOC] Quizlet's 2017 study plan: test date in, session plan out, with reminders and self-monitoring of progress. — [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2017-03-09-quizlet-adds-new-study-plan-feature-to-help-students-study-smarter)

### Inferences
- A common priority stack emerges across Anki, RemNote and Math Academy: (1) items in the learning/relearning phase today; (2) due reviews, exam-scoped first; (3) weak or demoted concepts; (4) new material, throttled by backlog; (5) optional review-ahead. New material is the adjustable term; reviews are the fixed obligation.
- A time budget is easier to reason about than card counts when item types differ in length (flashcards vs worked problems). Math Academy's XP = minutes convention, calibrated per task type and adjusted for the individual, gives both a daily goal and a completion-date forecast (remaining XP / daily XP), which is the same computation RemNote presents as an exam study plan.
- Daily exam goal = (reviews forecast to fall due before the exam + unseen items × expected first-day cost + final-pass items) / remaining study days; recompute daily; if the result exceeds the learner's budget, surface a catch-up choice (raise the goal, drop low-priority material, or accept lower target retention) rather than silently rescheduling, as RemNote does.
- Checkpoints tied to work done (quiz every 150 XP; knowledge check every 20 topics + 5 hours) give regular mixed retrieval practice and a calibration signal for the mastery model, independent of the item scheduler.
- Showing a decaying strength indicator needs care: it shifts effort from new learning to maintenance (Duolingo's "keep the tree gold"). Tie any decay display to a concrete, bounded action.

### Gaps
- No primary source was collected for Duolingo's current daily-goal tiers/XP values or its current session-generation rules.
- No vendor publishes how a time budget is split numerically between review and new material (e.g. a fixed percentage); the splits above are structural, not numeric.
- Per-review time costs (seconds per flashcard review, per lapse) used by the Anki simulator were not retrieved.

## 8. Published evaluations of scheduling choices

### Takeaway
The strongest causal evidence is small in number: one classroom experiment (personalised spaced review: +16.5% vs massed, +10.0% vs generic spaced on a cumulative exam) and Duolingo's A/B tests (which moved engagement, not measured learning). Everything else is predictive-accuracy benchmarking, simulation, natural experiments, or observational surveys.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Classroom RCT-style comparison (within a real course, matched review time): personalised review +16.5% over massed study and +10.0% over generic spaced review on a cumulative post-semester exam. — [Lindsey et al. 2014](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797613504302/)
- [DOC] Duolingo A/B tests: HLR vs Leitner (≈1M students, 6 weeks): any-activity daily retention +0.3%, practice -7.3%; simplified HLR vs full HLR (3.3M students, 2 weeks): any activity +12.0%, lessons +1.7%, practice +9.5%. Outcome is next-day return, not learning. — [Settles & Meeder 2016](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)
- [DOC] Duolingo Birdbrain A/B tests: lessons built with Birdbrain "consistently helped our learners learn more" and increased lessons done and day-over-day return; no numbers in the post. — [Duolingo blog, 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain/)
- [DOC, secondary/unverified] A Technical.ly report quotes Burr Settles: content length +8.9% and time spent learning +3.1% in skills practice with Birdbrain. — [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/?p=4207)
- [DOC] MEMORIZE natural experiment on Duolingo logs (~5.2M user-word pairs): learners whose review timing was closer to MEMORIZE had lower empirical forgetting rates than those closer to uniform or threshold schedules; not randomised. — [Tabibian et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6410796/)
- [DOC] Hunziker et al.: user studies in vocabulary and species-recognition apps show the optimisation-based teacher outperforming "popular heuristic approaches" (no effect sizes in abstract). — [arXiv:1805.08322](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08322)
- [DOC] Reddy et al. 2016: Mechanical Turk experiment supports the predicted phase transition when new items are introduced too fast. — [arXiv:1602.07032](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07032)
- [DOC] Predictive benchmark on 10k Anki users: FSRS-6 beats SM-2 for 99.6% of users; each FSRS generation lowers log loss (v4 0.3726 → 4.5 0.3625 → 5 0.3561 → 6 0.3460 → 7 0.3401); personal optimisation beats default parameters for 84.3% of users. — [Expertium Benchmark](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html); [srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)
- [DOC] Simulation claims (no human-subject test): FSRS needs 20–30% fewer reviews than SM-2 for the same retention; descending-retrievability order is better for backlogs. — [ABC of FSRS wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS); [Anki 24.11 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/ankitects/anki/release/24.11)
- [DOC] PFA vs BKT on 4 tutor datasets: PFA slightly better on log-likelihood, BIC, r and A'. — [Pavlik et al. 2009](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED506305.pdf)
- [DOC] Elo with a simple uncertainty function performs close to more complex variants in Pelánek's experiments ("improvement is often small"). — [Pelánek 2016](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)
- [DOC] Medical students (observational): 1,700 unique Anki cards ≈ +1 Step 1 point (n = 72); daily Anki use correlated with Step 1 (n = 165, P = .039); one cohort comparison found no significant difference (223.71 vs 222.58). — [Deng et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26498443/); [Wothe et al. 2023](https://ijms.pitt.edu/IJMS/article/view/1549); [PMC10403443](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10403443)

### Inferences
- The only exam-score evidence for a scheduling choice supports two things: spacing review over the term instead of massing it per unit, and personalising which items get reviewed using a fitted memory model. The incremental gain from personalisation (10 points of "boost" over generic spacing) was about 60% of the total gain over massing, and was largest for the oldest material; this argues for model-driven selection of old material in a cumulative-exam planner.
- Prediction accuracy gains between scheduler versions are well measured, but no study links them to exam outcomes; the Duolingo experience shows prediction gains can coincide with flat or negative behavioural effects. Scheduler upgrades should be validated on the planner's own outcome metrics (quiz scores at known dates are available to this team as a ground truth).
- Known quiz/exam dates give a natural evaluation design that most products lack: predicted recall per concept on the quiz date vs observed quiz performance, for calibration of both FSRS and the mastery estimates.

### Gaps
- No A/B test or classroom study was found that evaluates: raising desired retention before an exam; a final review pass; maximum-interval caps tied to an exam date; exam-aware vs exam-agnostic scheduling; or any FSRS configuration against exam scores.
- No independent (non-vendor) evaluations were found for RemNote's Exam Scheduler, Math Academy's FIRe, Khan mastery challenges or ALEKS knowledge checks in the sources retrieved; vendor statements are descriptive.
- Effect sizes from Hunziker et al. and the full Lindsey et al. results tables were not retrieved.
